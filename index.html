What This Project Is About

Built as an independent high school student research project exploring the intersection of extreme heat, rural medical facility access, and neurological health in North and East Texas.

Extreme summer heat is not just uncomfortable. For older adults and people living with neurological or chronic conditions (such as epilepsy, migraine, multiple sclerosis, stroke, dementia, and Parkinson's disease), high temperatures can trigger symptoms or interfere with how common medications regulate sweating and hydration.

Using public climate, Census, health, and hospital data across 24 North and East Texas counties (4 urban DFW core counties and 20 surrounding rural counties) and 1,885 Census tracts, this project examines where frequent 100°F+ heat overlaps with vulnerable populations and long driving times to emergency rooms, primary/EMS care, and specialized stroke centers. Each county is scored, mapped, and paired with a live forecast tool and a 1-page resident safety guide.

Methodology and Outputs

All charts, map layers, and county scores were built from public datasets using reproducible Python scripts.

1 Data Collected

Pulled Census ACS (age 65+, no vehicle), CDC PLACES (stroke), HRSA AHRF (physicians), and 1,885 tract centers.

expand_stage_a.py DATA_CHECKLIST.md
2 Heat Analyzed

Selected nearest NOAA stations (≥95% complete, 2015 to 2024) and counted annual days ≥100°F.

expand_stage_b.py noaa_tmax_expanded.csv
3 Drive Times Routed

Geocoded 327 Texas ERs and 114 DSHS stroke centers; routed population-weighted drives across 1,885 tracts.

expand_stage_c.py route_dshs_stroke.py tract_access.csv
4 Scored and Ranked

Merged 24 counties, ranked 1 to 24 on 7 measures, and averaged Heat, Vulnerability, and Access gap.

merge_expanded.py county_data_expanded.csv score.py
5 Published Outputs

Built the interactive map, 24-county report, live NWS lookup tool, 1-page guide, and policy brief.

map.html checker.html (live NWS tool) report_expanded.html resident_guide.pdf

Key Findings at a Glance

Highest Combined Risk: Hill County (19.89 / 24)High heat (19.4 days ≥100°F), high vulnerability (20.3% age 65+), 0 neurologists, 22.3 min average ER drive, and 80.9 min average drive to a DSHS Level I/II stroke center.
Rural vs. Urban Care-Access GapAverage population-weighted ER drive is 19.3 min in rural counties vs. 9.1 min in the urban core (Van Zandt averages 42.3 min). Average DSHS Level I/II stroke-center drive is 63.5 min rural vs. 17.4 min urban.
Specialist Deserts12 of the 20 rural counties have 0 neurologists on record (HRSA AHRF 2023), making primary care, EMS, and local ERs the primary frontline for heat and brain emergencies.
Though Government Programs Exist, Gaps RemainState and federal programs (CEAP cooling aid, 2-1-1 Texas, HeatRisk, HRSA Telestroke) provide real support, yet national heat alerts do not account for ER or specialist travel times, and telestroke only begins after hospital arrival.

Peer-Reviewed Evidence and Study Limitations

Supporting StudiesLongitudinal research links extreme outdoor heat (≥103°F heat index) to higher dementia incidence in neighborhoods with low AC access (Alzheimer's & Dementia, PMC13688717), faster cognitive decline in lower-income older adults (BMJ JECH 2023), and attention drops from warm indoor home temperatures (Hebrew SeniorLife 2025).
Key LimitationsThis is an ecological county-level study: it shows association, not causation. One NOAA weather station stands in for each county (Limestone was excluded for incomplete 10-year data), driving routes use OpenStreetMap roads from Census tract centers, and stroke centers reflect Texas DSHS designations rather than private outpatient clinics.
Informational only, not medical advice.
