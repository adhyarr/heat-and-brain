Heat and the Brain: Heat Exposure vs. Neurological Care Access in Rural Counties Near Dallas

HOSA project draft, updated 2026-11-17. All five measures now have data. Results are preliminary and the sources in Table C must be opened and checked.

1. Research Question

In rural counties near Dallas, which areas combine high heat exposure with low access to neurological and emergency care, and which patient groups are most at risk?

2. Hypothesis

Rural counties with more extreme-heat days, fewer neurologists and farther emergency rooms will have higher risk for people with neurological conditions.

9. Method: Heat-Risk and Access-Gap Score

  1. Rank each county on each measure (1 = lowest risk, 5 = highest risk). More heat days, older population, more households without a vehicle, higher stroke rate, fewer neurologists per 100,000, and longer ER distance all rank as higher risk.
  2. Average the ranks within three groups: Heat (days over 100°F), Vulnerability (age 65+, no vehicle, stroke rate), and Access gap (neurologists per 100,000, ER distance).
  3. Average the three group scores with equal weights to get the total. Equal weights are a simple starting choice and should be tested against other weights.
  4. Map the score in QGIS or Google My Maps and choose the 1–2 highest-risk counties for case studies.

  • Navarro has the highest total. It has no neurologists, the highest stroke rate, and moderate-to-high heat.
  • Wise has the longest drive to an ER (6.6 miles, about 16 minutes) and the second-most heat days among the rural counties.
  • Dallas has the most heat days but the best neurological access and the shortest drive to an ER.
  • Henderson has the oldest population (22.4% age 65+) but the fewest heat days.
  • All ER distances are short (2–7 miles), so ER distance separates the counties less than neurologist access does.
  • These patterns show association only.

12. Limitations

  • Shows association, not causation.
  • One weather station stands in for each county. Henderson's station (Athens) has missing days, so its heat count may be too low. Dallas's station is in the city, which adds urban heat.
  • Neurologist counts are very small (0–2) in rural counties, so one doctor moving changes the rank. Residents may also see neurologists in neighboring counties.
  • Stroke is one condition and stands in for neurological burden. It differs little between counties (2.9–3.7%).
  • ER distance is measured from each county's geographic center, not from where people live. It uses OpenStreetMap routing, which may differ from Google Maps.
  • The CMS hospital list left out Wise County's hospital, so Medical City Decatur was added by hand. Three candidate hospitals could not be located, and some locations came from a geocoder that was not checked by hand.
  • Equal weights in the score are a choice, not a finding.
