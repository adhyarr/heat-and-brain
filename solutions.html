Government Initiatives & Actionable Solutions

An overview of existing Texas and federal heat-mitigation programs, remaining last-mile access gaps, and recommended interventions.

1. Existing Government Initiatives

Both state and federal agencies allocate substantial funding and operate established programs for summer cooling assistance and rural stroke care.

Texas State Programs
  • CEAP / LIHEAP Cooling Assistance (TDHCA): Texas allocates roughly half of its federal LIHEAP energy funds through the Comprehensive Energy Assistance Program (CEAP) to summer electricity bills, crisis disconnection prevention, fans or air-conditioning units, and HVAC repair via approximately 30 local agencies.
  • 2-1-1 Texas & Local Cooling Centers (HHSC / TDEM): Dialing 2-1-1 connects residents to city and county cooling centers (libraries, senior centers, and community halls), emergency shelters, and utility assistance.
  • Texas DSHS Stroke Designation: Certifies hospitals across four stroke levels (Level I Comprehensive through Level IV Acute Stroke Ready) to standardize emergency transfer protocols.
U.S. Federal Programs
  • NIHHIS / Heat.gov & CDC–NWS HeatRisk: A multi-agency portal (NOAA, CDC, HHS, and FEMA) providing the 24-hour color-coded HeatRisk index alongside the county-level CDC Heat & Health Tracker.
  • HHS LIHEAP Weatherization & Cooling: Federal block grants assisting low-income older adults and individuals with disabilities in affording home air conditioning and summer utility costs.
  • HRSA Telestroke Grants (TNGP): Funds 24/7 video neurology networks connecting rural emergency departments with vascular neurologists at major stroke hubs for rapid imaging review and acute treatment decisions.
2. The Remaining Last-Mile Gap Despite substantial federal and state investment in HeatRisk, CEAP, and Telestroke, this 24-county, 1,885-tract analysis identifies three persistent last-mile gaps:
  • Heat alerts do not account for care deserts: National HeatRisk forecasts do not incorporate the fact that 12 of 20 rural counties have zero neurologists on record, or that 100% of residents in 8 rural counties live more than 60 minutes from a Texas DSHS Level I–II stroke center.
  • Telestroke operates only after hospital arrival: It does not reduce lengthy out-of-county emergency drives (such as Van Zandt County's 42.3-minute population-weighted average) or transportation barriers for households without a vehicle (6.2% in Hill County).
  • Daily medication and home-cooling gaps persist: Many older adults prescribed diuretics, blood-pressure medications, or anticholinergics do not receive proactive summer heat-medication counseling prior to extreme-heat events.

3. Recommended Interventions

Targeted measures combining individual and caregiver preparedness with local, state, and health-system policy.

Individual, Caregiver & Community Measures
  • Pre-summer medication review: Consult a physician or pharmacist before May to identify medications that impair sweating, thirst, or blood pressure regulation (diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, anticholinergics, antipsychotics, stimulants, and tricyclic antidepressants).
  • Temperature-safe medication storage: Keep epilepsy, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's, and blood-pressure medications away from hot vehicles and direct sunlight.
  • Twice-daily wellness checks: Monitor adults aged 65+ and individuals with dementia during ≥100°F heat waves, particularly in counties with high senior shares (Hood 25.4%, Rains 24.2%, Henderson 22.4%).
  • Advance cooling and emergency planning: Record 2-1-1 Texas and county transit numbers before summer, and call 911 immediately for confusion, slurred speech, or body temperature above 104°F.
Local, State & Federal Policy Measures
  • Integrate HeatRisk with care-access metrics: Prioritize emergency outreach and cooling resources in high-heat, high-access-gap counties such as Hill, Navarro, Erath, and Hood.
  • Expand routine tele-neurology: Extend HRSA telehealth funding to scheduled outpatient video neurology visits, reducing 60–90+ minute summer travel for rural older adults.
  • Provide 2-1-1 cooling transit & mobile stations: Fund on-demand transit vouchers for car-free seniors and deploy temporary cooling stations in rural towns lacking municipal centers.
  • Prioritize CEAP cooling aid & rural EMS supplies: Expedite HVAC and air-conditioning assistance for residents with neurological conditions and equip rural EMS units with active cooling supplies.

4. Additional Community & Health-System Solutions

Practical approaches that leverage existing rural infrastructure—pharmacies, volunteer fire stations, utilities, and primary care—without waiting for new hospitals.

Community & Clinical Touchpoints
  • Pharmacy heat-safety labeling: Rural towns without a neurologist often still have a local pharmacy. Adding summer heat-warning labels and brief pharmacist counseling when dispensing diuretics, anticholinergics, antipsychotics, or anti-seizure drugs reaches high-risk patients directly.
  • Micro-cooling hubs (volunteer fire stations & churches): Pre-registering air-conditioned volunteer fire halls, churches, and community centers with 2-1-1 Texas provides cooling access in unincorporated rural areas outside city limits.
  • Spring primary-care & CHW checklists: Distributing a 1-page heat-safety plan during routine spring appointments helps patients with MS, epilepsy, migraine, or dementia prepare before 100°F weather arrives.
EMS, Utility & Registry Coordination
  • Pre-hospital EMS stroke bypass protocols: In counties with 60–110+ minute drives to Level I–II stroke centers (such as Hill, Navarro, Erath, and Freestone), pre-hospital stroke screening helps EMS route severe strokes directly to thrombectomy-capable centers or coordinate air transport.
  • Heat-triggered utility shutoff moratoriums: Automatically pausing residential electric disconnections whenever NWS issues a Heat Advisory or Excessive Heat Warning protects older adults before a crisis occurs.
  • Voluntary EMS & Meals on Wheels check-in registries: Coordinating local 911/EMS, Meals on Wheels drivers, and home-health aides around voluntary medical registries enables proactive wellness checks during multi-day heat waves.
