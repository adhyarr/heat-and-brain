Extreme Heat & Brain Health: Quick Resident Guide

Practical, evidence-based safety steps for older adults and people living with neurological conditions in North & East Texas.

Emergency Warning (Call 911 Immediately): Heat stroke is life-threatening. Watch for confusion, slurred speech, fainting, dizziness, a body temperature above 104°F, or hot/flushed skin. Move the person to shade or AC, remove excess clothing, and cool them with water or ice packs while waiting for EMS.

Condition-Specific Heat Precautions

Epilepsy Dehydration, electrolyte loss, and overheating can trigger seizures. Stay in AC during peak heat, drink fluids regularly, store seizure medicines away from heat, and use extra caution around open water.
Multiple Sclerosis (MS) Heat can temporarily worsen fatigue, vision, and weakness (Uhthoff's phenomenon). Use cooling vests, cold drinks, or cool baths, and plan outdoor tasks for early morning or evening.
Migraine Heat and fluid loss are common triggers alongside bright sunlight. Hydrate with water or electrolytes before feeling thirsty, wear polarized sunglasses, and avoid peak midday glare.
Stroke & High Blood Pressure Heat makes the heart pump harder and can shift blood pressure or thicken blood from dehydration. Limit outdoor activity between noon and 3 p.m. and review diuretics or BP medicines with your doctor.
Dementia & Memory Loss People with dementia may not notice thirst or overheating and face serious danger if wandering outdoors. Offer water and hydrating foods often, keep living spaces cool, and secure exits.
Parkinson's Disease Parkinson's and some anticholinergic medicines can impair sweating and blood pressure control. Watch for dizziness or weakness, use AC, and consider telehealth visits during heat waves.

Medications & Hydration (CDC Guidance)

  • Never stop prescribed medications on your own, ask your doctor or pharmacist for a summer heat-medication plan.
  • High-risk drug classes: diuretics ("water pills"), ACE inhibitors, ARBs, anticholinergics, antipsychotics, stimulants, and tricyclic antidepressants.
  • If you have fluid limits: ask your doctor how much water is safe during heat waves.
  • Protect medicines: store inhalers, insulin, and pills in a cool, dry place, not a hot car.

Rural Access & Local Cooling Help

  • Find a cooling center or ride: dial 2-1-1 (Texas Health and Human Services) or check your county emergency management website for free local cooling centers and senior transit.
  • Don't rely on fans alone: when indoor temperatures pass the 90s, electric fans cannot cool the body enough, seek air-conditioned spaces (libraries, senior centers, community halls).
  • Ask about telehealth: if your nearest neurologist is 45–90+ minutes away, ask your clinic about video check-ins during extreme-heat advisories.
  • Buddy check: check on older neighbors or relatives at least twice a day during heat waves.
