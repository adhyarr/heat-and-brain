10-year heat trend (2015–2024)

Extreme-heat days surged across all 24 counties in 2022 (39.3-day average) and 2023 (43.1-day average), nearly four times the 2015–2016 baseline.

Only years with ≥90% daily station observations are plotted (Hood 2024 omitted for missing days). In 2023, Hood reached 62 days and Hill reached 55 days ≥100°F.

Spotlight: highest-risk rural case studies

Three rural counties illustrate distinct heat-and-access gaps.

Hill County (#1 overall · 19.89/24)

High risk across every dimension: 19.4 days ≥100°F/yr (55 in 2023), 20.3% age 65+, 6.2% no vehicle, and 0 neurologists. Most residents use Hill Regional Hospital in Hillsboro (avg ER drive 22.3 min), but 100% live >60 min from a DSHS Level I–II stroke center (avg 80.9 min).

Hood County (#4 overall · 15.78/24)

Both the hottest (34.2 days ≥100°F/yr; 62 in 2023) and oldest (25.4% age 65+) county in the study. While Lake Granbury Medical Center (Level III Primary Stroke) is 19.7 min away on average, residents average 55.7 min to a Level I–II comprehensive/advanced stroke center.

Van Zandt County (#8 overall · 14.00/24)

Longest emergency drive in the study: 42.3 min (28.0 mi) average to the nearest ER, with 100% of residents >15 min away. With no in-county CMS ER hospital, tracts route out-of-county to Kaufman, Quitman, or Athens, plus 0 neurologists and 20.2% age 65+.
